How the model works
  1. Data. A GitHub Action runs twice a week and pulls the Official World Golf Ranking plus every completed PGA Tour event leaderboard from ESPN's public feeds, plus a hand-maintained log of DP World Tour results and official European points snapshots. Everything lands in docs/data/model_output.json, which this page reads.
  2. Ratings. Each player's week is measured in strokes per round vs that field's average, weighted by event strength, recency (26-week half-life) and rounds played, then shrunk toward their OWGR-based prior. The result is a single number: strokes per round vs an average PGA Tour field.
  3. Team prediction. A selection score blends form, world rank, qualifying points, Ryder Cup experience and recent momentum. Early in the race, form and rank dominate; as 2027 results arrive, weight flows to the official points races (America's points start at The American Express in January, Europe's began at the British Masters in August 2026). A Monte Carlo of the actual selection process — 6 automatic qualifiers by projected points, then 6 captain's picks that favour experience — turns scores into probabilities.
  4. Match engine. Each player becomes a per-hole scoring distribution (eagle/birdie/par/bogey rates) from their rating. Matches are then simulated hole by hole, 25,000 at a time, producing win probabilities and exact scoreline distributions. A calibration constant sharpens per-hole gaps so a 2-stroke favourite wins ~77% of singles matches, matching real match play. Four-ball takes the best ball of two; foursomes blends partners' ratings with an alternate-shot penalty.
  5. Lineups. The 12×12 singles matrix is solved with the Hungarian assignment algorithm: the board that maximises USA points, the board that minimises them (Europe's ideal), and each player's dream and nightmare opponent.
  6. The Cup. 10,000 full 28-point Ryder Cups are simulated — 16 team matches with the suggested pairings, 12 singles, Europe at home — producing the win probabilities and the scoreline distribution on the Overview tab.

Making it your own

Fork it, tweak the knobs in model/config.py (match sharpness, home advantage, form half-life, selection weights), edit the seed pool in data/seed_pool.json, or add results to data/manual/results_log.json, then run python3 -m model.pipeline. The weekly GitHub Action recomputes everything and commits the fresh output automatically — so the model's opinions evolve with every tournament, and its git history is a diary of the 2027 season changing its mind.
